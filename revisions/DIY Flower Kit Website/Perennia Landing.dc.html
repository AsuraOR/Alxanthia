How it works

Four steps, twenty minutes, no tools

01 — Unpack

Lay out the plate

Parts come bagged and numbered against the illustrated plate, so you can see the whole flower before you start.

02 — Shape

Bend the petals

Fold each pre-cut length along the marked curve. Fingers only — the wire holds whatever shape you give it.

03 — Assemble

Wind onto the stem

Layer petals around the crown, twist, then add leaves. A drop of the included glue locks it.

04 — Keep

Place it anywhere

No water, no light, no wilting. Dust it once a season and reshape a petal whenever you like.

The material

Why pipe cleaner behaves like a real petal

A soft chenille pile over a twisted steel core: it takes a curve the way a petal does, and then it keeps it. That is what makes these flowers feel grown rather than folded.

  • i

    Reusable. Take a bloom apart and build it again — the parts don't tear.

  • ii

    Child-friendly. Every wire end is folded and sheathed before it leaves us.

  • iii

    Lasts years. No fading in a vase, no dropped petals, no water to change.

About Perennia

We began by cutting petals at a kitchen table, one species at a time, until the pieces fit together well enough to hand to a stranger.

Every kit is still cut, counted, and packed by hand here in Indonesia. New species are added to the catalogue as they are drawn and tested — four today, and more on the way.
